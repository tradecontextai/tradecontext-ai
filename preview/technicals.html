TOPIC 01
What is technical analysis?
FOUNDATION
Reading price and volume to figure out the highest-probability next move.

The three assumptions

  • Price discounts everything. All known information is already in the price. You don't need to read the news to read the chart.
  • Price moves in trends. Once a direction is established, it tends to persist longer than people expect.
  • History repeats. Patterns of human behaviour — fear, greed, FOMO — leave footprints on the chart that look the same across markets and decades.

What TA is good at

  • Defining entries, stops and targets with precision
  • Identifying trend regime — uptrend, downtrend, range
  • Finding support & resistance where reactions are likely

What TA is bad at

  • News-driven gaps. No technical signal predicts an Iran headline at 2pm.
  • Regime shifts. Patterns that worked all year suddenly stop when the macro regime flips.
⚠️ Pro principle

Technicals define where. Fundamentals define whether. The professionals use both — a clean technical entry only after the fundamental thesis lines up.

TOPIC 02
Trend & market structure
FIRST PRINCIPLE
Higher highs and higher lows = uptrend. Until that breaks, trade with it.

The classic definition

  • Uptrend — a series of higher highs (HH) and higher lows (HL).
  • Downtrend — a series of lower highs (LH) and lower lows (LL).
  • Range — neither pattern; price oscillates between a ceiling and a floor.

The trend rule

The trend is intact until a structural break — an uptrend ends when price prints a lower low. This single rule keeps you out of countless bad trades. "Don't fight the trend" isn't a cliché; it's mathematically supported by every backtest ever run.

Multi-timeframe alignment

Big money looks at at least two timeframes. A common setup: identify the trend on the daily, find the entry on the 1-hour. If the daily is up but the 1H is down, you're either fading a pullback or about to get crushed.

HH-HL-HH-HL = clean uptrend

TOPIC 03
Moving Averages
TREND FILTER
A single line that smooths the chart and tells you which side to take.

SMA vs EMA

  • SMA (Simple Moving Average) — average of the last N closes. Slow and smooth — great for higher timeframe trend.
  • EMA (Exponential) — weights recent prices more heavily. More responsive — preferred by short-term traders.

The classics worth knowing

  • 20-EMA — short-term trend; price above = bullish bias on the daily.
  • 50-SMA — medium-term. The line most institutional desks watch on equities.
  • 200-SMA — the king. Above = secular bull market. Below = secular bear. Self-fulfilling because everyone watches it.

Golden cross / Death cross

When the 50 crosses above the 200, it's the "golden cross" — bullish trend confirmation. The opposite is the "death cross." These are lagging signals but they're powerful because they affect institutional asset allocation.

TOPIC 04
RSI — momentum oscillator
MOMENTUM
A 0–100 score for how stretched price is.

What it measures

RSI (Relative Strength Index) compares the size of recent up-moves to recent down-moves over a 14-period window. The result is a number 0–100:

  • > 70 — overbought, momentum extended
  • < 30 — oversold, sellers exhausted
  • 50 — neutral midline

The common mistake

Most beginners short the moment RSI hits 70 and lose money. In a strong trend, RSI can stay overbought for weeks. Overbought ≠ "about to fall." The signal is when RSI crosses back through the level.

Where it really shines: divergence

The highest-quality RSI signal is divergence: price makes a new high but RSI doesn't. Means momentum is weakening even though the chart looks fine. Bearish divergence at a key resistance is one of the cleanest reversal trades there is.

RSI oscillates between extremes

TOPIC 05
MACD — trend & momentum together
CLASSIC
Two moving averages plotted as a single oscillator. Stupidly powerful for what it is.

The pieces

  • MACD line — difference between the 12-EMA and 26-EMA. Above zero = uptrend; below zero = downtrend.
  • Signal line — 9-EMA of the MACD line itself.
  • Histogram — the gap between MACD and signal. Expanding = momentum accelerating; contracting = momentum fading.

The two signals that matter

  • Bullish cross — MACD crosses above the signal line below zero. Trend-reversal signal.
  • Zero-line cross — MACD crosses above zero. Confirms the trend has flipped.

MACD divergence

Same idea as RSI — when price makes a new high but MACD doesn't, you have weakening momentum at the top. Combined with a resistance level, it's a high-probability fade.

TOPIC 06
Volume — confirmation, not prediction
VALIDATION
Price tells you what happened. Volume tells you whether to believe it.

What strong volume confirms

  • Breakouts — a breakout on heavy volume is real. On thin volume, usually a fakeout.
  • Trend continuation — pullbacks should happen on lower volume than the impulse moves.
  • Reversals — a "key reversal candle" on huge volume often marks the turn.

Volume divergence

If price keeps making new highs but volume keeps declining, the move is running out of fuel. Smart money is exiting; retail is the bag-holder. Very common just before a top.

The VWAP shortcut

For intraday traders, the VWAP (Volume-Weighted Average Price) is the level institutions use. Above VWAP = bullish session; below = bearish. Reverts to VWAP often.

⚠️ Forex caveat

Spot forex doesn't have real volume — it's decentralised. You can use tick volume as a proxy but it's not the same. Volume signals work much better on stocks, futures and crypto.

TOPIC 07
The classic chart patterns
PRICE ACTION
Shapes the crowd has watched for a century — and still watches.

Reversal patterns

  • Head & Shoulders — three peaks, middle one highest. Break of the "neckline" confirms reversal.
  • Double top / Double bottom — two peaks/troughs at the same level. Failed retest = reversal.
  • Inverse H&S — same as H&S but at a bottom. Cleanest bullish reversal pattern.

Continuation patterns

  • Flags & pennants — brief consolidation after a strong move. Break in the trend direction usually targets the same distance as the prior leg.
  • Ascending / descending triangles — flat ceiling + rising floor (or vice versa). Breaks tend to honour the flat side.
  • Cup & handle — rounded bottom + small flag. William O'Neil's classic bullish continuation.

The truth about patterns

Patterns don't work in isolation — they're probability shifts. A head-and-shoulders at major resistance + bearish divergence + risk-off macro = high-conviction trade. The same pattern in a roaring bull market with no resistance is a fade waiting to happen.

TOPIC 08
Bollinger Bands & ATR — volatility tools
VOLATILITY
Measure how "stretched" or "compressed" the market is — and size stops accordingly.

Bollinger Bands

Plotted as a 20-period moving average with bands ±2 standard deviations above and below. The bands widen when volatility expands and contract when it falls.

  • Band squeeze — the bands narrow to historic lows. Almost always followed by an explosive move (direction unclear from the bands alone — watch for the breakout direction).
  • Walking the band — in a strong trend, price rides the upper or lower band. Don't fade it.
  • Mean reversion at extremes — only in ranging markets, a poke outside the band tends to revert to the mid line.

ATR — the trader's friend

ATR (Average True Range) measures average daily range. It's the single best tool for sizing stops. Rule of thumb: place your stop 1.5–2× the ATR away from entry — gives the trade room to breathe through normal noise without getting stopped on a wick.

Position size should scale inversely with ATR. High-ATR market = smaller size to keep risk constant. Most blow-ups come from using the same lot size on a quiet day and a volatile one.
