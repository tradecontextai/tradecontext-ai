TOPIC 01
What is fundamental analysis?
FOUNDATION
Looking at the value behind the price — the cash, the policy, the politics.

The core idea

Fundamental analysis values a market by what's actually happening in the underlying economy or company — interest rates, growth, profits, debt, geopolitics — and asks whether the current price reflects it. If it doesn't, you have an edge.

The two flavours

  • Macro fundamentals — used for forex, indices, commodities and crypto. Drivers: GDP, inflation, employment, rates, central-bank policy, geopolitical risk.
  • Microeconomic fundamentals — used for individual stocks. Drivers: earnings, revenue growth, margins, guidance, balance-sheet health, valuation multiples.

Why it matters

Fundamentals are the why. A trader who only watches the chart sees what moved. A trader watching fundamentals sees what's about to. Most blow-ups happen when traders ignore an economic release and a "clean technical setup" gets destroyed by an unexpected number.

TOPIC 02
The big macro indicators
MACRO · DAILY DRIVERS
If you only watch four numbers, watch these.

Most days, the market is reacting to one of a small set of macro releases. Learn what they are and you'll stop being blindsided.

CPI · Inflation
Released monthly · 13:30 UK / 08:30 ET
The single biggest data point in markets right now. Hotter-than-expected CPI = central banks stay hawkish = bonds sell, dollar rallies, gold & tech wobble.
NFP · Non-Farm Payrolls
First Friday of the month · 13:30 UK / 08:30 ET
US jobs growth + wages. A strong print signals economic resilience but stickier inflation — usually bullish for the dollar, bearish for rate-sensitive assets.
GDP · Growth
Quarterly · advance, revised & final readings
The big picture: is the economy expanding or contracting? Two negative quarters = recession. Drives the medium-term equity/bond regime call.
PMI · Business Surveys
Monthly · manufacturing + services
A reading above 50 = expansion, below 50 = contraction. Leading indicator — moves before GDP confirms it. Pay special attention to Germany & China.
Retail Sales
Monthly · US, UK, EU
The consumer is ~70% of most developed economies. Strong retail = strong economy = the central bank can stay tight. Weak = recession risk rising.
Unemployment Rate
Monthly · alongside NFP in the US
Low unemployment + rising wages is the inflation engine — central bank stays hawkish. A sudden rise often signals the cycle is rolling over.

TOPIC 03
Central banks & interest rates
THE BIGGEST DRIVER
The most powerful traders in the world don't trade — they set the price of money.

Why rates rule everything

Interest rates are the cost of money. When they rise: bonds get more attractive, equities get repriced lower (the discount rate is higher), the currency strengthens (carry trade), and growth stocks suffer most. When they fall, the opposite. Almost every long-term market regime is anchored on rate expectations.

The big four banks to watch

  • Fed (US) — sets the global tone. Watch the FOMC statement, the press conference, and crucially the "dot plot" (committee members' rate projections).
  • ECB (Eurozone) — drives EUR; second-most-watched.
  • BoE (UK) — drives GBP; usually trails the Fed.
  • BoJ (Japan) — for decades the dovish outlier. Any signal of tightening sends USD/JPY tumbling and global volatility surging.

Hawkish vs dovish

  • Hawkish — favouring higher rates / tighter policy → bullish for the currency, bearish for risk assets.
  • Dovish — favouring lower rates / looser policy → bearish for the currency, bullish for risk assets.
⚠️ Pro tip

Trade the expectation, not the decision. Markets price in the most likely outcome days before the meeting; the move happens when reality differs from what was priced.

TOPIC 04
Earnings & company data
STOCKS · MICRO
For individual stocks, four times a year the truth comes out.

What gets reported

  • EPS (Earnings per Share) — profit divided by shares outstanding. Beat consensus → usually a pop. Miss → usually a drop.
  • Revenue — top-line sales. Growth here matters more for early-stage names.
  • Guidance — management's forecast for next quarter / year. This drives 70% of the post-earnings move.
  • Margins — gross + operating + net. Expanding margins = efficient business. Contracting margins = warning sign.

Valuation multiples that matter

  • P/E ratio — price ÷ earnings. ~15–20 is "normal" S&P; >30 is expensive; >50 is priced-for-growth.
  • P/S ratio — for unprofitable growth names that don't have P/E yet.
  • Forward P/E — based on next year's expected earnings. Better than trailing.

The earnings playbook

Strong stocks often run into earnings (price-action premium), then trade the reaction. Watch the 1-min chart in after-hours right after the print — that's where the real fight between bulls and bears plays out.

TOPIC 05
Geopolitical risk
VOLATILITY · TAIL RISK
Wars, elections, sanctions — slow to build, fast to unwind.

The flight-to-safety reflex

When geopolitical risk spikes, capital flows out of risk and into "safe havens": gold, US Treasuries, USD, JPY, CHF. The classic combo: equities sell, gold rallies, oil reacts both ways (supply fear vs demand destruction).

Where to watch

  • Middle East — affects oil, gold, the dollar via safe-haven flows.
  • Russia / Ukraine — affects European gas, EUR, defence stocks, food commodities.
  • Taiwan strait / South China Sea — affects semiconductors (TSMC), AUD, regional risk premium.
  • US elections + trade policy — affects USD, tariffs-sensitive stocks, foreign exporters.

How to trade it

Don't try to predict events. React fast: watch a live news feed, score the impact, then trade the established safe-haven correlations. A clean Iran-related headline at 2pm + a long gold position is one of the most repeatable trades in the macro playbook.

TOPIC 06
Reading the economic calendar
PROCESS
Your single most important tool. Free, accurate, predictive.

The three columns that matter

  • Forecast (or "consensus") — what economists expect.
  • Previous — last release.
  • Actual — what just came out (the surprise vs forecast is what moves price).

How to filter the noise

  • Filter by impact: high. Anything else is mostly background.
  • Filter by your traded currency / region. If you trade NQ, you care about US data + Fed speakers.
  • Mark 30 minutes before any high-impact release — that's "no new positions" time.

The surprise effect

The move scales with the surprise, not the absolute number. CPI 3.1% vs 3.1% forecast = nothing. CPI 3.3% vs 3.1% forecast = big move, even though 3.3% by itself is unremarkable.

TOPIC 07
Market sentiment indicators
RISK-ON / RISK-OFF
The crowd's emotional state — measured.

The big four sentiment gauges

  • VIX — the "fear index." 30-day implied volatility on the S&P 500. <15 = complacent, 20–30 = normal, >30 = panic.
  • CNN Fear & Greed Index — composite 0–100. Extreme greed = contrarian sell signal; extreme fear = contrarian buy signal.
  • DXY (Dollar Index) — when DXY rallies hard, it's risk-off (dollar = safe haven). When it falls, risk-on.
  • 10Y Treasury yield — rising = expectations of growth + inflation; falling = expectations of slowing growth.

Risk-on vs risk-off in one line

  • Risk-on: stocks ↑ · crypto ↑ · gold flat/down · AUD/JPY ↑ · VIX ↓
  • Risk-off: stocks ↓ · gold ↑ · DXY ↑ · USD/JPY mixed · AUD/JPY ↓ · VIX ↑
